## Spreadsheets are not the enemy

A spreadsheet is an excellent tool. Plenty of businesses run for years on one summary file and one group chat. Problems creep in slowly, as the number of people, records and rules grows while the tool stays the same. This article helps you judge whether you have passed that point.

## Six signs it is time to switch

### The same data is typed more than once

A request is noted in the chat, copied into a spreadsheet and typed again into a report. Every copy is a chance for a typo, and nobody knows which copy is right.

### Numbers cannot be traced to where they came from

The month-end total differs from what was expected, and finding the cause takes hours. An overwritten formula, a deleted row or a cell showing #REF! leaves no trace.

### Approvals get lost in conversations

"Has this been approved yet?" becomes a daily question. Approvals given in a chat are hard to find again, especially when the person who gave them is on leave or has moved to another team.

### Nobody knows which file is the latest

There is report FINAL, report FINAL (2) and report FINAL revised. Two people edit different files on the same day, and one of them has to redo the work.

### Only one person understands it

Only one person knows the formulas and the order of the work. When that person is ill or leaves, the work stops too.

### Mistakes show up too late

Stock turns out to be empty just when the item is needed. Pay turns out to be short after payslips have gone out. A mistake found late always costs more than one caught when the data is entered.

## Examples from real projects

### Payroll for outsourced staff

A company that places its staff with many clients has to calculate pay with different rules for every contract. Paydays differ, staff can start mid-period, social security deductions differ by position and cash advances are repaid from salary. In a spreadsheet, every rule becomes a formula that has to be looked after by hand. In the system I built, every pay line shows base pay, active days, the social security deduction and the cash advance that reduced it, so admins can check the total line by line.

### Stock requests and procurement

Work units request items, the warehouse keeps stock and procurement buys from vendors. When the three keep separate records, a request can be approved for items that are not there. In the system, an urgent request is checked against stock straight away. If there is not enough, the system creates a procurement for the shortfall only. When the goods arrive, the waiting request completes by itself.

## When a spreadsheet is still enough

Not every job needs a system. A spreadsheet is still the right choice when

- only one or two people use the data,
- the rules are simple and rarely change,
- there is no multi-level approval,
- small mistakes are easy to spot and cheap to fix.

Building a system for work like this adds cost without a matching benefit.

## A safe first step

You do not have to replace everything at once. Pick the one workflow that goes wrong most often or takes the longest, and move that one first. Changes are rolled out in stages so daily work keeps running, and old data is moved carefully so nothing is lost.
