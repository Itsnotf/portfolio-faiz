## Why there is no fixed price

Custom software is built around the way your business works, not the other way round. Two apps that are both called payroll software can be very different inside. One only multiplies base pay by the days worked. The other has to follow a different payday for every contract, deduct social security by position and repay staff cash advances from their salary. Same name, very different work.

So a more useful question than "how much does it cost" is "what does this system need to handle". The answer to that question is what sets the cost.

## The five factors that matter most

### 1. The number of workflows

A workflow is the sequence of steps from start to finish, for example an item request from a work unit that is checked against warehouse stock and then passed on to purchasing. Every workflow needs its own screens, rules and testing. A system with one workflow is much lighter than one that combines requests, stock and procurement.

### 2. The number of roles and approval levels

Who uses the system, and who is allowed to approve what? Admins, staff and department heads each see different screens and buttons. Multi-level approval adds work, especially when there are rules for revisions. In one of my projects, follow-up reports went through seven review stages based on the reviewer's position, and every revision request had to return to the reviewer who asked for it rather than to the first stage.

### 3. How complex the calculation rules are

Rules that are clearly written down are easy to build. What takes time are the exceptions, such as staff who start mid-month, deductions that differ by position, or loans repaid from salary. Every exception has to be asked about, built and then tested with real numbers so the result is right to the last cent.

### 4. Old data that has to be moved

If the data is spread across several spreadsheets in different formats, it has to be tidied before it goes into the system. Think of item types typed freely so that one item has three spellings, or prices stored as text. Tidying that without losing anything is a job of its own.

### 5. Reports, dashboards and printed documents

Every PDF report, payslip or dashboard chart needs designing and its numbers checked. A report that must match a format the company already uses usually takes longer than one with a new format.

## Factors people often forget

- **A mobile app.** If people in the field need an app on their phone as well as the web version, the work grows.
- **Connections to other systems**, such as cameras, attendance devices or software you already use.
- **AI features** such as face recognition. These are only worth it when the problem really calls for them, because they need more testing.
- **Support after launch.** Fixes and small changes can continue under a support agreement.

## How to get an accurate estimate

Before asking for a price, prepare these three things.

1. Describe the workflow from start to finish, including who does what.
2. Collect examples of the documents you use today, such as spreadsheets, forms or payslips.
3. Write down which part goes wrong most often or takes the longest.

With that, I can map the workflow, point out the most complex parts and then give a cost and time estimate for each stage. Payment is split by stage too, so every payment matches work you can already see.

## How to keep the cost down without cutting quality

- Start with the one workflow that causes the most trouble, and add others once the first is really in use.
- Choose a simpler new report format when the old one is not required.
- Start tidying your old data now, a little at a time.
